# Theory

Develop, synthesize, or critique a theoretical account so that its competing explanations, mechanistic link, and critical test are explicit and grounded in current local evidence. Route study architecture to `experiment-design`, measurement questions to `methods`, and quantitative specification to `statistics`.

---

## 1. Theory Navigation Protocol

When entering this mode, establish the theoretical scope:
1. **State the focal theory/construct** (e.g., "memory reactivation via gaze reinstatement", "age-related differences in source memory").
2. **Identify the relevant framework layer**:
   - *Cognitive* (episodic memory, working memory, attention)
   - *Neural* (hippocampus, MTL, PFC contributions)
   - *Computational* (pattern completion/separation, predictive coding)
3. **Locate current evidence locally first:** verified literature-wiki source notes, then their reference-trimmed local MinerU evidence. Read current project authorities only when applying the account to a live study. Archived material is provenance, never current authority.

---

## 2. Construct Mapping

When a construct is introduced, map it through the full chain:

```
Construct → Theoretical Claim → Operationalization (Variable) → Measure (DV/IV)
```

Keep theoretical claims distinct from candidate observables. Do not invent project variables, thresholds, or predicted directions. When a construct is converted into a test, hand the claim and competing predictions to `experiment-design`.

---

## 3. Theory Development & Thinking

Use these moves when building, evaluating, or refining a theoretical account:

- **Abduction first**: Start from the phenomenon, not the mechanism. "What would have to be true for this result to occur?" generates hypotheses; "what does the theory predict?" only tests them.
- **Distinguish levels**: Separate computational (what is computed), algorithmic (how it is computed), and implementational (neural substrate) claims — confusion across levels is a common source of circular reasoning.
- **Name the critical test**: A good theory should make at least one prediction that, if falsified, would require revising the core claim. Identify it explicitly.
- **Check the mechanistic link**: State the specific assumption that connects manipulation → process → outcome. That link is often where theories are vague and where studies are underpowered to test.
- **Moderators:** Treat age, context, and population differences as hypotheses about mechanisms, not universal assumptions. Ground them in evidence and the live study population.
- **Critical tests:** State what observation would favor one account over another, then route design and operationalization decisions to their owning skills.

---

## 4. Literature Integration Protocol

When discussing a specific theoretical claim:
1. **Anchor the claim** to the primary source(s) — cite by citekey (e.g., `author2012topic`, `author2014topic`).
2. **State the level of evidence**: single study, replicated, meta-analyzed, theoretical proposal.
3. **Identify moderators** — does the effect replicate across age groups? In VR? With different modalities?
4. **Surface counterevidence** and plausible alternative mechanisms.
5. **Map to observables conditionally** without treating a convenient measure as identical to the construct.

Every concept evidence-table claim has a manuscript-ready citation sentence plus a 3–5 sentence detail narrative covering sample, hardware/method, statistics, and theoretical implication.

---

## 5. Theoretical Gap Analysis

To identify a gap or novel contribution:
1. **Describe what is known** (established effects).
2. **Describe what is unknown or untested** (the gap).
3. **State the specific prediction** your study makes.
4. **Identify the mechanistic assumption** that links the manipulation to the outcome.

---

## 6. Measurement boundary

Theory may state why multiple indicators should reflect a construct. `methods` evaluates measurement validity and `statistics` evaluates scale, composite, and invariance implications.

---

## 7. Cross-Vault Synthesis

When synthesizing across multiple sources:
1. Search verified LLM Wiki source and synthesis notes.
2. Open reference-trimmed local MinerU evidence when claim-level support must be checked.
3. Use `zotero-manager` only to diagnose missing evidence or discover library items; use web search only when local and Zotero evidence are insufficient.
4. Synthesize and cite without copying bulk evidence into the vault.
5. Update a live project authority only when the task authorizes that application.

---

## 8. Destination-specific synthesis

When the authorized destination is another vault or repository, read its nearest `AGENTS.md`, live templates, and current authority before editing. This skill supplies theoretical reasoning and evidence boundaries; it does not impose Research paths or a fixed section schema on another workspace. Keep each note focused, define the construct early, connect claims to operationalizations and primary evidence, and preserve the destination's native structure.

## Verification

- Each claim is anchored to a citekey and labelled with its level of evidence.
- Counterevidence and plausible alternative mechanisms are stated.
- The critical test names the observation that would favor one account over another.
- Theoretical claims stay distinct from candidate observables; no project variable, threshold, or predicted direction was invented.
- Evidence was read locally before any Zotero or web lookup.
