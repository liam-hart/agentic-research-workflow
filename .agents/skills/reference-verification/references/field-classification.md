# Bibliographic field classification

Use this reference for batch audits or when records disagree.

## Identity-threatening discrepancies

Treat these as proposed corrections or human-decision items supported by explicit authority:

- the DOI resolves to a different title or first-author identity;
- core title terms identify a different work;
- authors are missing, added, or reordered relative to the final authoritative record;
- publication type, journal/proceedings, article number, or pagination identifies another work;
- the entry combines fields from online-first and final records into a state that no authority reports.

## Usually non-identity differences

Classify rather than automatically repair:

- title capitalization or punctuation;
- journal abbreviation versus full title;
- middle initials and name-presentation conventions;
- online-first year versus issue year;
- issue omitted where the target style does not require it.

The target citation style controls presentation only after identity and final publication state are settled.

## Ambiguity rules

- Prefer the publisher or official proceedings record for final author order and publication status; use Crossref as strong corroboration and DOI-target validation, not universal final authority.
- Distinguish page ranges from article numbers and e-locators.
- Preserve retractions, corrections, versions, preprints, and conference-to-journal relationships as different bibliographic identities unless an authority explicitly links them.
- If two authoritative records disagree, retain both values and links in `conflict among authorities`.
- If no appropriate authority can be reached, use `not verified` rather than a guessed correction.
