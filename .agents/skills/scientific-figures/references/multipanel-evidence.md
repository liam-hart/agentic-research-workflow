# Multi-panel evidence architecture

Use this reference when planning, revising, or auditing a labelled multi-panel manuscript figure.

## Necessity test

Write the figure-level claim, then ask of each panel:

1. What part of the claim would become unsupported, ambiguous, or overgeneralized if this panel disappeared?
2. Does the panel establish the effect, show its dependence structure, qualify a boundary, test an alternative explanation, or provide a necessary validation?
3. Is it genuinely distinct evidence, or the same result redrawn with another summary?

Panels that do not survive this test are candidates for removal, combination, another figure, or supplementary material. Placement remains controlled by the manuscript and venue authority.

## Evidence map

For each panel record:

| Panel | Subclaim or role | Data and unit | Approved analysis/summary | Dependency | Source-data location | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Dependencies include shared samples, repeated measures, derived variables, or a result that cannot be interpreted before another panel. Preserve these relationships in panel order and visual encoding.

## Escalation check

The figure claim must not exceed its strongest necessary panel. If a robustness, subgroup, or exploratory panel changes the scope of the claim, return that decision to the manuscript and statistical authorities rather than solving it with layout.
